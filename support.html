Howzat support

Help with Howzat 1.13, the cricket arcade game for iPhone and iPad. Some features below are not available in earlier versions.

Contact support

For help, bug reports or privacy questions, email aagrawal207@gmail.com.

From version 1.13, you can also write from the app: choose Settings › About › Send Feedback. It opens a draft email to the developer in your own mail app, with the subject "Howzat feedback". A short footer gives the app version, build number, device model and iOS version, so problems are easier to fix. You can edit or remove that footer, and nothing is sent unless you send the email yourself. If no mail app is available, Howzat shows the address instead, with Copy Email Address.

You can also use Howzat's public GitHub issues. Posting an issue requires a GitHub account.

GitHub issues are public. Include only details you are comfortable sharing publicly, such as your iPhone or iPad model, iOS version, app version and steps to reproduce the problem. The app version is in Settings › About.

How to play

Howzat runs on iOS 17 or later. On iPhone, hold the phone sideways. On iPad, play in any orientation and any window size. An upright iPad or a narrow window shows a smaller field, with a hint to turn the iPad sideways or widen the window.

On first launch, a short lesson opens by itself and asks you to hold the pad, slide and release. You can use Skip lesson or practise again through Learn the pad on the main menu.

  1. Use the trackpad. Drag inside the wide pad to pull the bat. Move back for a backlift, forward to swing, or down to defend a low ball. Raising the target raises the hands; the wrists and body guide the blade angle. Touches outside the pad do not steer.
  2. Release to recover. Lift your finger to let the bat and batsman settle back into guard.
  3. Hit the numbered rails. A struck ball can score 1, 2, 4 or 6 runs. Each ball scores once. The two small numbered gaps in the ceiling's OUT rail are dropped catches worth 1 and 2 runs.
  4. Defend the wicket. Any ball hitting the stumps or bail is BOWLED. Your shot can be CAUGHT at a charcoal OUT rail until it next touches the ground. After that bounce, an OUT rail absorbs the ball for no runs. The bat touching the stumps or bail is HIT WICKET.

Your score is your runs. Strike rate is an informational statistic and does not multiply your score. Reaching 50, 100, 200 or 300 runs, and then every hundred up to 700, triggers a milestone celebration: the batter raises the bat to the crowd while play continues.

What is the duck?

If you are out for 0 runs, a small duck pops up at the batter's feet and waddles off before the scorecard appears. Out on your first ball is a golden duck. The scorecard shows OUT FOR A DUCK or GOLDEN DUCK. The duck is only for show; the innings is recorded as usual.

With Effects on, the duck quacks as it pops up. From version 1.13, a golden duck says "quack quack".

Why did the ball become faint?

Each ball allows one bat strike. After that strike it becomes faint and passes through the bat and other balls. It can still score or hit the wicket. Resting balls fade away so the next delivery can arrive.

Can I score behind the wicket?

Yes. An angled, open blade can glance a ball toward the FOUR or SIX rails behind the batter. The ceiling above that area is still OUT, so lofting too high is risky.

Why does the game look different?

From version 1.13, Howzat is styled like a county-ground match programme and scorebook: printed paper pages, a green scoreboard with ivory number plates, and ticket-stub buttons. Settings, Scores and Your player follow your device's appearance; in Dark Mode they use a darker "evening edition". The controls, scoring and bowling are unchanged.

On most iPhones with Face ID, the live scoreboard also fills the thin strip above the field, so its figures are larger. The field itself is the same size as before.

Controls and footwork

  • Adaptive footwork is the default. The batsman steps forward or back as you move the bat.
  • Planted footwork keeps a fixed stance. Choose Settings › Batting › Footwork › Planted if you prefer it.
  • Bat size defaults to 95% and is adjustable from 75% to 125% in Settings › Batting. A bigger bat covers more space.
  • Playing side puts the pad in the left or right bottom corner and mirrors the field and bowling direction. Choose Settings › Batting › Playing side.

Bat size and playing side can change from the menu or while paused. The pause screen's gear opens Settings; closing Settings leaves the innings paused until you choose Resume. Footwork changes apply at the next innings.

How is Settings laid out?

Tap the gear on the menu or the pause screen. When there is room, Settings shows two columns: Batting, Career and Privacy on the left, and Sound and feel, About and My Other Apps on the right. Smaller iPhones, narrow iPad windows and the largest text sizes show one column. Each section ends with short notes that explain its settings, one sentence each.

  • Batting: Bat size, Playing side, Footwork and Pause after boundaries.
  • Sound and feel: Effects and Crowd switches, a Music volume slider, Tracks and Haptics.
  • Career: Game Center sign-in, innings finished, total runs, time played and whether your progress is saved to iCloud.
  • Privacy: Share anonymous usage.
  • About: Send Feedback, Support Development, Rate Howzat, Acknowledgements and the app version.
  • My Other Apps: App Store links to the developer's other apps.

Before version 1.13, Pause after boundaries had its own Between balls section.

Your player's look is not in Settings. It has its own screen, described below.

Your player

Tap the portrait button beside the gear, on the menu or the pause screen, to open Your player. You cannot open it during live play. It is laid out like a cricket-card album: each choice is a numbered card, and a look you have not earned yet is an empty slot.

  • Player has Face, Skin, Hair and Headwear. All eight faces are free for everyone. Every face works with every skin tone, hair style, hair colour and headwear choice.
  • Kit has Looks, Shirt, Pads, Gloves and Bat. Looks are matching sets you can wear in one tap once all four pieces are unlocked. Each kit piece unlocks by earning an achievement or a career goal. None can be bought, and tips do not unlock them.

Each tap saves at once. A locked piece can be tried on without saving, and the caption shows what earns it. A gold dot on the portrait marks new looks. Your look does not change how the bat plays.

Which hair styles are there, and where is facial hair?

Hair has ten styles: Short, Braid, Ponytail, Bun and Long tied, plus Afro, Curly, Long curls, Dreadlocks and Buzz cut, which are new in version 1.13. All are free and work with every face, hair colour and headwear choice.

From version 1.13, the moustache and beard choices are in the Hair tab, below the styles and colours. The Classic, Round, Strong and Long faces offer them; other faces show a short note naming those four. If you switch to another face, your saved facial hair is kept and shows again when you return to a face that offers it.

Bowlers and innings

Each six-ball spell can be Spinner, Medium or Fast, including repeated styles. Spinner deliveries can use standard spin, topspin or backspin, which changes their pace off the pitch. The scoreboard shows the speed of each delivery when it is bowled.

Medium and Fast bowlers also bowl short balls that bounce up towards chest height. Medium bowlers bowl them more often; spinners never do. Leaving or ducking a short ball is usually safe, but a top edge can be caught off the ceiling.

Bowling gets harder as your innings grows. Past 50, 100 and 200 runs, and then every 50 runs up to 700, deliveries come quicker within each bowler's pace range, fast bowlers appear more often and spinners turn the ball more. From 100 runs, Medium and Fast bowlers mix in slower balls, and from 200 runs they also bowl yorkers. Pace never goes above 150 km/h.

A new delivery waits until the previous ball has gone. Fours, sixes and milestones add a wait before the next ball while you can still move the bat. You can turn off the waits after fours and sixes in Settings › Batting › Pause after boundaries; milestones always pause.

The sky changes as your innings goes on. Most innings start in the afternoon and move through golden hour and dusk to a floodlit night. Some start at night and move through dawn to morning. The sky holds still for the first 12 balls and reaches its last stage at ball 60.

Can I resume an innings after closing the app?

Yes. Howzat saves your innings between balls, when you pause and when the app leaves the screen. If the app closes, is updated, or you choose Main Menu from pause, the menu offers RESUME INNINGS with your runs, balls and place in the spell. Play picks up after a short READY.

A ball still in the air when the app closed counts as a dot: one ball faced, no runs and never out. A wicket ends the innings at once, so closing the app cannot undo it.

New innings or Learn the pad asks first. If you start a new one, the saved innings is recorded as Retired, and its runs count toward your career and best.

Sound, music and haptics

Settings › Sound and feel has Effects, Crowd and Haptics switches and a Music volume slider. Effects are the bat, ball and stumps. Crowd is the stadium noise and cheers; it continues through dismissal, the final scorecard and replay, and stops in the main menu, during pause and READY, and in the background. Music plays background tracks on the menu, while you bat and on the scorecard. Slide Music all the way to the left to turn it off. While music plays, the crowd is a little quieter.

Settings › Tracks chooses one track or a shuffle, which tracks are in the shuffle, and lets you preview any track. If you play your own music, Howzat's music stays quiet and the game sounds mix with yours.

If you cannot hear the game, check these switches and the Music slider, your device's volume and Silent mode. Howzat respects Silent mode. Its cricket recordings are bundled with the app and its music is generated on the device, so both work offline.

Scores and Game Center

Tap the trophy to open Scores:

  • Career shows your best innings, your top five, your best this week, the 12 achievements with your progress, and local lifetime statistics. Detailed totals cover completed innings saved with a full scorecard.
  • Bowlers compares your results against Spinner, Medium and Fast. Its painted pitch heatmap shows first-bounce length, with Runs / ball, Balls and Outs views. Tap a band or use the previous/next buttons to inspect its exact results.
  • Recent shows the latest 10 of up to 20 saved innings. Recorded career totals continue after older cards leave the list.
  • Worldwide shows optional Game Center rankings. This week ranks each player's best innings this week and restarts every Monday at 00:00 UTC; the first season starts on 12 October 2026. Best innings ranks all-time personal bests. Total runs and Innings rank lifetime runs and finished innings from your first game. Average ranks career runs per innings for players with at least 20 recorded innings. Versions before 1.6 also had a Strike rate board.

Career, Bowlers and Recent work offline. Bowler analysis covers completed innings recorded with that feature; abandoned innings and older history do not fill in missing results. To use Worldwide, connect to the internet and choose Sign in to Game Center in Scores or Settings if needed. An account already signed in to Game Center can submit scores automatically. You can play the whole game without Game Center.

After an innings, the scorecard is a scorebook page: your runs on number plates, a short line on how you were out, such as "Bowled by the Fast bowler off your 12th ball.", and your balls, strike rate and shots. It can also tell you how close you came to your best. Share opens the share sheet with a matching scorebook-page image of that innings. Achievements you have already earned, such as a past century, are credited from your saved records.

Earlier installs may have only partial score history. Missing dates, balls or shot counts are shown as unavailable rather than guessed or treated as zero.

What is last week's top 10?

From version 1.13, at the start of a new week (Monday 00:00 UTC), the main menu may show last week's top 10 from the This week leaderboard, once: ranks, Game Center names and runs. Your row is gold if you made the ten; otherwise the card shows your rank and runs. See this week opens Scores on Worldwide › This week, and Done returns to the menu. The names come from Game Center and are not saved or sent anywhere.

You might not see it because:

  • You didn't bat last week. Game Center offers the ended week's board only to players who submitted a score that week.
  • You aren't signed in to Game Center. The card never asks you to sign in. Sign in from Scores or Settings to see it in a later week.
  • It shows once per week. After you have seen it, it waits until next Monday.

It also waits for a quiet main menu: it never covers an innings, the pause screen, the scorecard, an open sheet or the first pad lesson. If Game Center is slow or fails, it tries again at a later menu that week. A new install waits until its second week.

How does iCloud sync work?

From version 1.12, when iCloud is on, Howzat saves your progress to your own iCloud account. Your iPhone and iPad share one career: scorebook, totals, bests, achievements, earned looks and your player's look. Progress also comes back after you delete and reinstall the app, or move to a new device, when you sign in with the same Apple Account. Totals add up across your devices without counting an innings twice.

Some settings stay on each device, because you may hold an iPhone and an iPad differently: bat size, playing side, footwork, sound and music, haptics, pause after boundaries and the pad lesson. A saved unfinished innings also stays on the device where you played it.

If iCloud is off, everything still works and your progress is saved on this device only. Settings › Career says which applies. To turn iCloud off for Howzat, use the iOS Settings app.

Why does strike rate not change with every delivery?

The bracketed number on the scoreboard counts balls faced. A ball counts once its result is decided: when it scores, is caught or bowled, comes to rest, or leaves the field. Strike rate uses that count at the latest scoring or dismissal event and is rounded down to two decimal places.

Optional tips and reviews

Settings › About › Support Development offers five one-time tip options through Apple's In-App Purchase system. Tips are optional and unlock no content or features. Apple handles payments and purchase history; Howzat keeps a local verified-transaction record for the thank-you count.

The tip sheet uses a light paper background in either device appearance. Larger accessibility text places each price below its explanation; scroll to read all five options. On iPad, the sheets fit any window size.

After five consecutive days with a completed innings and an innings of at least 50 runs, Howzat may request Apple's review sheet after you return to Main Menu. Requests are at least 120 days apart, limited to two in a rolling 365 days, and require a newer app version. Apple decides whether to show the sheet and does not tell Howzat whether you rated the game.

See the privacy policy for the local records used by these features, the iCloud copy of your progress and how data deletion works.
